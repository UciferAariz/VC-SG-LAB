1. Why we need precise instruments

The least count (LC) of an instrument is the smallest length it can measure. A reading can never be more precise than the LC, so the LC sets the least count error of every measurement.

InstrumentTypical least countGood for
Metre scale1 mm = 0.1 cmlengths of a few cm or more
Vernier callipers0.1 mm = 0.01 cmdiameters, internal diameters and depths of a few cm
Screw gauge0.01 mm = 0.001 cmwire diameters and sheet thicknesses below a few mm

2. Vernier callipers

Principle and least count

A vernier has N divisions that together are slightly shorter than N main-scale divisions. In the usual vernier, 10 vernier scale divisions (VSD) = 9 main scale divisions (MSD).

1 VSD = (9/10) MSD  ⇒  LC = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = (1/10) MSD = 0.1 mm = 0.01 cm.
In general: N VSD = (N − 1) MSD  ⇒  LC = MSD / N.

How to read

  1. Main scale reading (MSR): the last main-scale mark to the left of the vernier zero.
  2. Vernier scale reading (VSR): the number n of the vernier mark that lines up best with any main-scale mark.
  3. Observed reading = MSR + n × LC.

Zero error

Close the jaws with nothing between them. If the vernier zero does not sit exactly on the main-scale zero, the instrument has a zero error (ZE). The zero error is the reading shown with the jaws closed.

  • Positive zero error: vernier zero to the right of the main zero. If vernier mark n coincides, ZE = +n × LC.
  • Negative zero error: vernier zero to the left of the main zero. If vernier mark n coincides, ZE = −(N − n) × LC.

Zero correction = −ZE.   Corrected reading = Observed reading − ZE.

Worked examples

  1. LC = 0.01 cm, MSR = 2.3 cm, vernier mark 5 coincides, no zero error. Observed = 2.3 + 5 × 0.01 = 2.35 cm.
  2. Jaws closed: vernier zero is to the right and mark 3 coincides. ZE = +3 × 0.01 = +0.03 cm. A later observed reading of 4.16 cm gives corrected = 4.16 − (+0.03) = 4.13 cm.
  3. Jaws closed: vernier zero is to the left and mark 7 coincides. ZE = −(10 − 7) × 0.01 = −0.03 cm. Observed 4.16 cm gives corrected = 4.16 − (−0.03) = 4.19 cm.

The outer (lower) jaws measure external sizes, the inner (upper) jaws internal diameters, and the depth rod depths, all on the same scales.

3. Screw gauge (micrometer)

Principle: screw motion

The spindle is moved by an accurate screw. In one complete turn of the thimble the spindle moves forward by a fixed distance called the pitch. The thimble has N equal divisions (the circular scale).

LC = pitch / N, for example 0.5 mm / 50 = 0.01 mm.

To find the pitch, note a division on the datum line, turn the thimble exactly one full turn, and see how far the thimble edge moves along the linear (pitch) scale.

How to read

  1. Pitch scale reading (PSR): the last linear-scale mark uncovered by the thimble edge. Whole millimetres are above the datum line; on a 0.5 mm pitch gauge the half-millimetre marks are below it. If a half-mm mark is visible after the last whole mm, add 0.5 mm.
  2. Circular scale reading (CSR): the circular division on the datum line.
  3. Observed reading = PSR + CSR × LC.

Zero error

Bring the faces together with the ratchet, with nothing between them.

  • Positive zero error: the circular-scale zero lies below the datum line. If division n is on the datum line, ZE = +n × LC.
  • Negative zero error: the circular-scale zero lies above the datum line. If division n is on the datum line, ZE = −(N − n) × LC.

The numbers on the visible face of the thimble increase going upward, and opening the gauge moves the thimble surface downward past the datum line. That is why "zero below the datum line" means a positive zero error.

Backlash and the ratchet

Backlash: a worn screw has a little play. When you reverse the direction of turning, the thimble turns a few degrees before the spindle moves, so the reading is wrong by that amount. Always approach the final reading in the same direction.

Ratchet: the ratchet knob slips (and clicks) once the spindle touches the object, so the object is never squashed. If you over-tighten with the thimble itself, a soft wire is compressed and the reading comes out too small.

Worked examples

  1. Pitch 0.5 mm, N = 50 (LC 0.01 mm). The 2 mm mark and the half-mm mark after it are visible; division 23 is on the datum line. Observed = 2.5 + 23 × 0.01 = 2.73 mm.
  2. Faces touching: division 4 on the datum line, zero below it. ZE = +4 × 0.01 = +0.04 mm. Observed 0.76 mm gives corrected = 0.76 − 0.04 = 0.72 mm.
  3. Faces touching: division 46 on the datum line, zero above it. ZE = −(50 − 46) × 0.01 = −0.04 mm. Observed 0.76 mm gives corrected = 0.76 + 0.04 = 0.80 mm.

4. Errors and how to report them

Kinds of error

  • Systematic errors push every reading the same way: zero error, backlash, a worn instrument, parallax from viewing at an angle. They are removed by correction or better technique, not by averaging.
  • Random errors scatter readings both ways: small differences in how the object is held, slight imperfections of the object. Averaging many readings reduces them.
  • Least count error comes from the resolution of the instrument: no reading is better than ± 1 LC.

From readings to a result

For n corrected readings a₁, a₂, …, aₙ:

  1. Mean: ā = (a₁ + a₂ + … + aₙ) / n
  2. Absolute error of each reading: |Δaᵢ| = |ā − aᵢ|
  3. Mean absolute error: Δā = (|Δa₁| + … + |Δaₙ|) / n
  4. Relative error: δa = Δā / ā; percentage error = δa × 100 %

Reporting rule used in this lab: the uncertainty cannot be smaller than the least count, so Δa = the larger of Δā and LC. Round Δa to the decimal place of the LC and round ā to the same place. Write the result as a = (ā ± Δa) unit, for example d = (2.73 ± 0.01) mm. Keep extra decimals only in the working.

Example: readings 2.73, 2.74, 2.72, 2.73, 2.75 mm with LC 0.01 mm. ā = 2.734 mm, Δā = 0.0088 mm (smaller than the LC), so d = (2.73 ± 0.01) mm and the percentage error ≈ 0.32 %.

Combining errors (maximum-error rule)

  • Sum or difference, Z = A ± B: ΔZ = ΔA + ΔB.
  • Product or quotient, Z = AB or A/B: ΔZ/Z = ΔA/A + ΔB/B.
  • Power, Z = Aᵖ: ΔZ/Z = p · ΔA/A.
QuantityFormulaRelative error
Volume of a sphereV = (4/3)πr³ = (π/6)d³ΔV/V = 3 Δd/d

5. Precautions

Vernier callipers

  • Find the zero error with the jaws closed and apply the correction with its sign.
  • Hold the object gently; the jaws should just touch it, not press it.
  • Lock the slider with the locking screw before reading.
  • Keep your eye directly above the lines to avoid parallax.
  • Take readings at different positions and orientations; use the mean.

Screw gauge

  • Always finish with the ratchet, never by forcing the thimble.
  • Approach the final reading in one direction only (backlash).
  • Find the zero error with the faces just touching (ratchet clicking).
  • For a wire, measure at several points and in two perpendicular directions.
  • Do not forget the half-millimetre marks below the datum line.

6. Common exam traps

  • Sign of the zero error. Vernier zero right of main zero (circular zero below the datum line) = positive. Left (above) = negative.
  • Adding instead of subtracting. Corrected = Observed − ZE. A negative zero error makes the corrected reading larger.
  • n instead of N − n. For a negative zero error use −(N − n) × LC, not −n × LC.
  • Forgetting the half-mm mark on a 0.5 mm pitch screw gauge: the answer comes out 0.5 mm too small.
  • Division number instead of n × LC: writing 2.3 + 5 instead of 2.3 + 5 × 0.01.
  • Unit slips: mixing mm and cm makes the answer ten times too big or too small.
  • Too many decimals: a final result cannot be more precise than the least count.
